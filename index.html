📄Biology Notes / Unit 3

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which plants, algae and some bacteria convert light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose. It takes place mainly in the chloroplasts of leaf cells, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll.

The overall equation is: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2.

Light-dependent reactions

These reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy is absorbed by chlorophyll and used to split water molecules (photolysis), releasing oxygen as a by-product. The energy is captured in the form of ATP and NADPH.

The Calvin cycle

Also called the light-independent reactions, the Calvin cycle takes place in the stroma. The enzyme RuBisCO fixes carbon dioxide onto ribulose bisphosphate, and ATP and NADPH from the light-dependent stage are used to produce glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate, which is built into glucose.

Limiting factors

The rate of photosynthesis is affected by light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. Whichever factor is in shortest supply limits the overall rate.
